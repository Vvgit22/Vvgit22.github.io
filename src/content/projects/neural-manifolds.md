## Problem

Finding biomarkers of Alzheimer's disease in brain activity means finding low-dimensional structure in very high-dimensional recordings — here in human fMRI data.

## What I built

A year-long Imperial group project — the lab's first on human fMRI — applying manifold learning to the UK PREVENT Dementia cohort, supervised by Prof Simon Schultz. I am Project Manager of the 7-member team.

## How it works

We are refining MIMI, an information-theoretic method that infers low-dimensional structure in brain activity, and analysing how default-mode-network manifolds change with age across participants to find candidate Alzheimer's biomarkers. The interactive illustration on the demo page runs on synthetic data generated in the browser.
