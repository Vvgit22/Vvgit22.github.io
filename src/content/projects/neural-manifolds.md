## Problem

Finding biomarkers of Alzheimer's disease in brain activity means finding low-dimensional structure in very high-dimensional recordings — and doing it in human data, not models.

## What I built

A year-long Imperial group project — the lab's first on human fMRI — applying manifold learning to the UK PREVENT Dementia cohort, supervised by Prof Simon Schultz. I am Project Manager of the 7-member team.

## How it works

We are refining MIMI, an information-theoretic method that infers low-dimensional structure in brain activity, and analysing how default-mode-network manifolds change with age across participants to find candidate Alzheimer's biomarkers.

## What this does and does not show

The interactive illustration on this page runs on synthetic data generated in your browser. No PREVENT Dementia data and no Schultz Lab results are shown. The project started in October 2026; there are no results yet.
